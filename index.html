The point of no return

At the event horizon, escape velocity exceeds the speed of light. Everything crossing it is gone from this atlas forever. The glowing disk is matter taking its time to fall in.

One galaxy among two trillion.

13.8 billion years, in order.

Scroll sideways. Each card is a moment where the universe became noticeably more interesting.

0 seconds

The Big Bang

Space itself begins expanding. Everything that will ever exist is packed into a region smaller than an atom.

380,000 years

First light

The universe cools enough for atoms to form. Light travels freely for the first time. We still see it as the cosmic microwave background.

200 million years

First stars ignite

Giants hundreds of times the mass of the Sun burn bright and die fast, seeding space with heavier elements.

1 billion years

Galaxies assemble

Gravity pulls stars into spirals and ellipses. The Milky Way's oldest halo stars date from this period.

9.2 billion years

The Sun switches on

A cloud collapses in the Orion arm. Leftover dust becomes eight planets and a lot of loose rubble.

10.0 billion years

Life on Earth

Single cells appear in the oceans. Nothing much happens for the next three billion years, which is fine.

13.8 billion years

You, reading this

An atlas of the whole thing fits in your pocket. The photons hitting your eye from this screen are the newest light in the universe.
